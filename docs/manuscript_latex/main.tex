\section{Abstract}
Local-first software architectures let devices read and write local data while disconnected and synchronize later. CRDTs provide convergence for particular replicated data types under their stated assumptions; that does not mean every conflict-resolution rule preserves an application's intended meaning. For relational data, semantic interpretation, integrity constraints, and recovery behavior must be evaluated separately.

We describe SemanticSync, a prototype SQLite replication engine with three-way conflict detection, tracked operation replay, and schema-assigned pairwise resolution policies (\texttt{\detokenize{SUM}}, \texttt{\detokenize{BOUNDED_SUM}}, \texttt{\detokenize{AVG}}, \texttt{\detokenize{APPEND}}, \texttt{\detokenize{LWW}}, \texttt{\detokenize{MIN}}, \texttt{\detokenize{MAX}}, and \texttt{\detokenize{REJECT}}). The implementation also includes tested quarantine paths for dependent rows and checkpoint-assisted log compaction. These mechanisms do not constitute a general CRDT convergence proof or a complete relational replication guarantee.

The saved SET-based stress artifacts contain two paired trials for each of nine configurations, using the same generated trace within each pair and alternating execution order. The workload performs sequential tracked writes followed by batch synchronization. Across configurations, SemanticSync's conflict-only SCR ranges from 97.50\% to 98.25\% (configuration scores, not a confidence interval); LWW scores 0\% under this workload's additive oracle. Reinspection of per-row records shows every SemanticSync SCR shortfall occurs when both replicas write the same non-base value: the state-diff detector treats equal values as no conflict, while the oracle counts both generated deltas. Thus the measured range includes this operation-intent mismatch and is not an unqualified SUM-correctness rate. The LWW result is specific to the additive oracle and does not establish general superiority. RVAR is 100\% for benchmark-row values in the observed schedules, not full-database agreement. With only two trials, timing summaries are descriptive; paired deltas vary in sign and magnitude, so they support no general performance-advantage claim. A separate 13-seed explicit-INCREMENT pilot matched its additive oracle in one 1,000-operation/5\%-conflict configuration; this is exploratory evidence, not a full-matrix or fair comparative-performance result. The older single-run matrix is reported separately as historical evidence.

\textbf{Keywords:} local-first systems, SQLite replication, semantic merge, conflict resolution, referential integrity, checkpoint compaction

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{The Local-First Relational Dilemma}
Local-first systems let users work with local copies and synchronize later. Relational replication adds concerns beyond conflict selection: updates may express different business intent, schemas impose row and referential constraints, and replicas may reconnect with delayed or incomplete histories. CRDT research establishes convergence for specified data types under stated delivery and causal assumptions \cite{shapiro2011,gomes2017}; it does not imply that every database replication system uses one model or that convergence alone establishes application-level correctness.

\subsection{The Convergence vs. Correctness Paradox}
Consider a warehouse management database with an \texttt{\detokenize{inventory}} table containing a \texttt{\detokenize{quantity INTEGER}} column. Suppose an item has an initial quantity of 100 in the common ancestor state:

\begin{itemize}
\item Replica A operates offline and sells 15 units (\texttt{\detokenize{UPDATE inventory SET quantity = 85 WHERE item_id = 1}}).
\item Replica B operates offline and sells 20 units (\texttt{\detokenize{UPDATE inventory SET quantity = 80 WHERE item_id = 1}}).
\end{itemize}
Under a last-writer selection rule, if Replica B is selected as the winner:

The resolved quantity is 80.

Both replicas may store 80 after a successful write-back, while the additive interpretation of the two independent changes is 65. This illustrates a mismatch between a selected merge rule and the assumed business meaning; it is not a claim about all CRDTs or all LWW systems.

Similar failures occur across application domains:

\begin{itemize}
\item In medical record collaboration, averaging sensor observations (\texttt{\detokenize{AVG}}) or appending multidisciplinary clinical notes (\texttt{\detokenize{APPEND}}) is overwritten by whoever happened to submit last.
\item In security-sensitive tables, concurrent modifications to a patient's blood type or an access control role are silently clobbered unless explicitly flagged for human inspection (\texttt{\detokenize{REJECT}}).
\item When a tracked parent delete arrives while dependent child rows are present, SemanticSync quarantines their payloads; a later child INSERT whose parent is absent is rejected for review. These outcomes preserve data/integrity for the tested paths, but do not automatically reconcile conflicting business intent.
\end{itemize}
\subsection{Key Contributions}
This artifact reports a prototype with schema-assigned, type-validated pairwise resolution policies; separate tracked absolute assignments and explicit increments; transactional quarantine on enumerated tracked parent-delete paths; and checkpoint-backed log pruning with ACK, membership, snapshot, history, and dependency checks. It also provides a synthetic stress harness and a separate exploratory increment-evaluation runner. These are implementation and evaluation contributions within the documented scope, not a claim of novelty over all related systems or a general convergence guarantee.

\subsection{Storage Layout and Tracked Operation Log}
Each replica maintains a SQLite database file with metadata for tracked operations, quarantine, compacted receipts, and checkpoint markers. Only supported tracked mutation paths create operation records; arbitrary SQL and external writes are not captured. Eligible history may be removed after checkpoint validation, so the hot log is not permanently append-only:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{\detokenize{_ss_change_log}}: A hot log of column-level mutations (\texttt{\detokenize{table_name}}, \texttt{\detokenize{row_id}}, \texttt{\detokenize{column_name}}, \texttt{\detokenize{old_value}}, \texttt{\detokenize{new_value}}, \texttt{\detokenize{ts_pt}}, \texttt{\detokenize{ts_l}}, \texttt{\detokenize{replica_id}}, \texttt{\detokenize{op_type}}, \texttt{\detokenize{origin_id}}, \texttt{\detokenize{sequence}}, \texttt{\detokenize{source_id}}, \texttt{\detokenize{depends_on}}, \texttt{\detokenize{bounded_delta}}, \texttt{\detokenize{bounds_min}}, \texttt{\detokenize{bounds_max}}).
\item \texttt{\detokenize{_ss_quarantine}}: An isolated holding table storing orphaned child rows (\texttt{\detokenize{quarantine_id}}, \texttt{\detokenize{table_name}}, \texttt{\detokenize{row_id}}, \texttt{\detokenize{row_payload}}, \texttt{\detokenize{parent_table}}, \texttt{\detokenize{parent_row_id}}, \texttt{\detokenize{reason}}, \texttt{\detokenize{quarantined_at}}, \texttt{\detokenize{status}}).
\item \texttt{\detokenize{_ss_compacted_receipts}}: Operation identities and payload hashes for pruned operations, retained as replay/duplicate evidence; this does not by itself establish complete causal history.
\item \texttt{\detokenize{_ss_checkpoint_metadata}}: Singleton tracking active checkpoint incarnation ID and snapshot epoch.
\end{itemize}
The tracked mutation API distinguishes an absolute \texttt{\detokenize{SET}}/\texttt{\detokenize{UPDATE}} from an explicit numeric \texttt{\detokenize{INCREMENT}}. An increment logs its signed delta under a stable operation ID and applies each received ID once; selected tests cover two-replica reordered delivery, duplicate relay after restart, and conflict review when an absolute SET races an increment. This narrow operation model is separate from the pairwise \texttt{\detokenize{SUM}} resolver and does not establish arbitrary N-way convergence. The saved historical stress matrix still uses absolute assignments and retains its previously documented additive-oracle mismatch; those measurements have not been regenerated using \texttt{\detokenize{INCREMENT}}.

\subsection{Hybrid Logical Clock Metadata}
Hybrid logical clocks were introduced to combine physical-clock proximity with logical causality and consistent-snapshot reasoning \cite{kulkarni2014hlc}. SemanticSync stores HLC fields with tracked operations and uses supplied timestamps in selected comparisons, but this implementation does not establish a network-wide happened-before relation: synchronization is local and pairwise, and general row-mutation history does not encode complete causal dependencies. HLC values are not used alone to prove checkpoint stability.

\section{Semantic Merge Policy Framework}
\subsection{Mathematical Formalization}
Let V denote the domain of values for an attribute. A three-way merge takes a base value and the values and metadata from two replicas; it returns a resolved value or a flagged divergence that requires manual intervention.

For a pairwise merge to be independent of which replica is passed as the first argument, the resolver must treat each value together with its replica identity and metadata. This pairwise symmetry is useful, but is not by itself a convergence proof: associativity, duplicate handling, retained operation identity, and delivery semantics also matter. \subsection{Tombstone Reconciliation (Update vs. Delete)}
When the detector reports an update/delete row conflict, the pairwise sync path supports table-level choices. \texttt{\detokenize{FLAG_FOR_REVIEW}} is the default and records a conflict without automatic write-back. \texttt{\detokenize{DELETE_WINS}} attempts a paired delete; a write failure leaves the conflict flagged. \texttt{\detokenize{UPDATE_RESURRECTS}} requires a dictionary row payload and attempts a paired upsert; absent or malformed payloads remain unresolved. These choices do not classify general causal ordering, and tests cover selected two-replica histories rather than arbitrary delivery schedules.

\subsection{Type Compatibility and Fail-Fast Validation}
Before runtime execution, policies assigned via \texttt{\detokenize{PUT /api/v1/databases/\{id\} /policies}} are validated against normalized SQLite column types. \texttt{\detokenize{SUM}}, \texttt{\detokenize{AVG}}, \texttt{\detokenize{MIN}}, \texttt{\detokenize{MAX}}, and \texttt{\detokenize{BOUNDED_SUM}} accept \texttt{\detokenize{INTEGER}}, \texttt{\detokenize{REAL}}, or \texttt{\detokenize{NUMERIC}}; \texttt{\detokenize{APPEND}} accepts \texttt{\detokenize{TEXT}}; \texttt{\detokenize{LWW}} and \texttt{\detokenize{REJECT}} use the registry wildcard. Incompatible types fail validation. The resolver's Python comparisons may accept more values in isolation than the policy validator permits for a configured column.

\section{Foreign-Key Quarantine Boundary}
In relational databases, foreign-key constraints define referential integrity invariants. In SQLite, this is enforced via \texttt{\detokenize{PRAGMA foreign_keys = ON}}.

\subsection{The Referential Cascade Problem}
Suppose Replica A inserts an \texttt{\detokenize{order_items}} record referencing \texttt{\detokenize{order_id = 10}} while Replica B deletes \texttt{\detokenize{orders}} record \texttt{\detokenize{10}}. If the insert arrives after the parent is gone, FK enforcement rejects the insert and reports it as a rejected operation. If a tracked delete is applied while dependent rows are present, the supported delete path quarantines those rows before removing the parent. Quarantine preserves payloads but does not automatically reconcile the conflicting business intent; an operator may need to review the insert or quarantined child.

\subsection{Quarantine Isolation Protocol}
For the supported tracked delete paths, SemanticSync uses a transactional quarantine boundary:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Connection Enforcement:} Tracked local writes, operation delivery, paired conflict resolution, and quarantine recovery open SQLite connections with \texttt{\detokenize{PRAGMA foreign_keys = ON}} before beginning their write transaction.
\item \textbf{Delete Handling:} Local deletes, received \texttt{\detokenize{DELETE}} and \texttt{\detokenize{RESOLVE}}-delete operations, and paired engine/API delete resolutions recursively identify declared dependent rows and serialize their payloads into \texttt{\detokenize{_ss_quarantine}} before deleting the parent.
\item \textbf{Atomic Validation:} Row removal, quarantine records, and the tracked operation/receipt share a transaction. \texttt{\detokenize{PRAGMA foreign_key_check}} is run before commit; an error rolls the transaction back.
\item \textbf{Parent-Key Updates:} These are not quarantined. SQLite's declared \texttt{\detokenize{ON UPDATE}} action applies (for example, \texttt{\detokenize{CASCADE}}) or the tracked transaction is rejected when the constraint blocks the update.
\item \textbf{Operator Recovery:} Quarantined rows can be relinked to an existing parent or marked \texttt{\detokenize{confirmed_deleted}} through transactional API handling, subject to the supported single-column parent-key and single-column-FK relink limits.
\end{enumerate}
These guarantees cover the tested tracked paths, not arbitrary database access. Direct SQL-console writes use FK-enabled connections but do not create operation identities or quarantine dependent rows, and do not use the shared mutation lock. SQLite's declared FK action or constraint rejection governs those writes. External processes can open connections with foreign keys disabled.

\section{Checkpoint-Assisted Log Compaction}
Uncompacted tracked-operation logs can grow over time and add replay cost; this implementation prunes only under the checkpoint conditions described below.

\subsection{Compaction Eligibility and Recovery Checks}
Compacting a change log prematurely can discard history needed by a lagging replica. In the implemented \texttt{\detokenize{/sync/compact}} path, an operation's presence in every enrolled replica's durable ACK vector is necessary evidence, but not sufficient authorization. The checks below describe the tested tracked-operation path, not a formal proof for distributed execution.

\subsection{Compaction Protocol}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{ACK and horizon evidence:} Recovery requires the committed manifest's member IDs to equal the complete currently enrolled replica set, and its ACK-vector keys to match those members. Candidate IDs must be present in every vector with the same non-null payload hash. The stable cutoff is the minimum, over replicas, of each replica's maximum HLC among acknowledged operations; each local log entry must be strictly below it. HLC is a cutoff after exact-ID evidence, never proof of delivery. Missing vectors or an empty common set retain the logs. A membership change after manifest commitment blocks that checkpoint before ACK reconciliation or pruning.
\item \textbf{Committed checkpoint evidence:} The explicitly initialized checkpoint store, store identity, committed manifest, full archived payload hashes, membership/incarnation/path data, and one-to-one snapshot set must validate. Snapshot file hashes, SQLite integrity, embedded identity, schema/data fingerprints, and exact operation/receipt frontier are recomputed. The live replica must match the snapshot plus a replay-validated tracked tail, and every acknowledged operation must be represented consistently in replica history.
\item \textbf{Durable ACK reconciliation:} Before pruning, recovery validates every member's ACK vector and registry evidence. ACK gaps from a committed manifest may be reconciled only after all members pass preflight; persistence is checked and failed in-memory updates roll back.
\item \textbf{Per-entry eligibility:} In each replica transaction, an entry must have a unique operation identity, match the acknowledged payload hash, and have dependencies either included in the eligible set or already represented by a compacted receipt. Entries at or above the strict HLC cutoff, duplicate identities, hash mismatches, and entries with unresolved dependencies remain in the hot log.
\item \textbf{Atomic local prune:} An eligible operation's compacted receipt and checkpoint marker are written atomically with its hot-log deletion in that replica's \texttt{\detokenize{BEGIN IMMEDIATE}} transaction. The checkpoint journal advances through \texttt{\detokenize{PRUNING}} to \texttt{\detokenize{COMPLETE}} only after all members succeed. A committed partial checkpoint can be retried idempotently; invalid or ambiguous evidence blocks the affected checkpoint without authorizing its pruning.
\item \textbf{Trust boundary and limitations:} \texttt{\detokenize{compact_change_log()}} is a low-level helper and trusts its caller-supplied eligible-ID set and cutoff; it does not independently verify the registry or checkpoint manifest. The production API builds those arguments only after the checks above. Direct Python callers are trusted internal code, not an independently safe authorization boundary. Coordination uses one process-wide lock; multiple processes and power-loss durability are not certified.
\end{enumerate}
\section{Empirical Evaluation}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}
The seeded stress harness creates private temporary SQLite databases. Each paired trial uses the same generated operation list, equivalent base rows and replica initialization, and one execution of each system; system order alternates by trial. It records Python version, platform, machine and processor strings returned by the runtime, SQLite runtime version, journal mode and FK setting, seed, workload parameters, trace digest, trial index/order, and policies. It does not infer storage-device identity or control system load. Writes run sequentially before batch sync, not concurrently.

The repository contains three evaluation paths:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Domain scenario endpoint:} It returns an \texttt{\detokenize{illustrative_unvalidated}} status and null comparative fields; former proxy-based outputs are excluded from empirical evidence (Section 6.3).
\item \textbf{Three-by-three parametric SET-based stress harness:} Scaled synthetic workloads across 1,000, 5,000, and 10,000 operations at 5\%, 20\%, and 50\% conflict densities (seed 42; two paired trials in the saved artifacts).
\item \textbf{Explicit-INCREMENT exploratory runner:} A separate operation-intent workload family. This manuscript reports 13 independent seeds at 1,000 operations/5\% density and one-trial 5,000/20\% and 10,000/20\% runtime-feasibility runs. It uses sequential local writes followed by batch pairwise synchronization; none is a concurrent-writer experiment.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Semantic Correctness Rate (SCR):} In the stress harness, the proportion of final values for generated conflict rows that match the independently constructed semantic oracle. Conflict rows are identified by the workload's \texttt{\detokenize{is_conflict}} flag, carried with each operation ID and written to the artifact as \texttt{\detokenize{conflict_row_ids}}. Both replicas' final values are scored. Non-conflict rows are excluded; the denominator is twice the number of conflict rows. A zero-conflict run has no SCR denominator and reports N/A (\texttt{\detokenize{null}} in JSON and blank in CSV).
\item \textbf{Benchmark-Row Value Agreement Rate (RVAR):} The proportion of ordered \texttt{\detokenize{bench_rows.value}} pairs that are equal across the two replicas. It excludes schemas, internal logs, receipts, quarantine records, and all other database state; it is not full-database convergence. A passing RVAR does not establish equal database contents.
\item \textbf{Valid Resolution Accuracy (VRA):} Percentage of conflict resolutions that satisfy domain range and referential integrity constraints. The synthetic stress workload defines neither such constraints nor a validity oracle, so stress VRA is reported as N/A.
\item \textbf{Stress Batch Sync Delta:} SemanticSync batch sync time minus LWW batch sync time. It includes the measured batch synchronization pipeline and is not isolated policy-execution time.
\item \textbf{Explicit-INCREMENT Pilot Delta:} Engine INCREMENT batch-sync duration minus a direct-SQL application SUM merge on the same trace. The direct path lacks equivalent receipts, conflict persistence, FK quarantine, and checkpoint guarantees; this is a diagnostic mechanism-cost contrast, not fair end-to-end overhead.
\item \textbf{Per-Operation Mutation Latency Percentiles (p50, p95, p99):} In the historical SET-based stress harness, the interval surrounds each replica-local \texttt{\detokenize{apply_change}} call, including SQLite mutation and change-log capture. In the explicit-INCREMENT pilot it surrounds \texttt{\detokenize{apply_increment}} for the engine and a direct-SQL transaction for the application path. Writes are sequential, not simultaneous. Summaries are runner-specific; the pooled pilot reports the distribution of trial-level write p95 values, not a pooled distribution of all raw calls.
\item \textbf{Batch Synchronization Duration:} Wall-clock time around \texttt{\detokenize{sync_replicas}} only; excludes database creation and tracked writes. Each system's durations and the paired SemanticSync-minus-LWW delta are recorded by trial and summarized in milliseconds. This is a system-level sync timing, not resolver-only overhead.
\end{itemize}
\subsection{Domain Scenario Runner (Not a Reproducible Result Set)}
The inventory, notes, and healthcare workload classes create SQLite fixtures and list expected conflict scenarios. The former API benchmark implementation scored the replica-B value as an LWW proxy, called \texttt{\detokenize{SemanticResolver.resolve}} directly without executing the replica synchronization engine, and emitted hard-coded convergence values. The current \texttt{\detokenize{/benchmark/run}} endpoint returns \texttt{\detokenize{evaluation_status="illustrative_unvalidated"}} with comparative metric fields set to null. Its output is not empirical evidence of comparative performance or convergence. Checked-in tests verify this status response and fixture setup, not domain scenario scores; no validated per-run domain result artifact is saved. The former domain SCR percentages, evaluation-loop timings, and convergence figures are excluded from empirical results. A valid domain experiment needs an independent oracle, actual policy and baseline execution, saved run records, and assertions over outcomes.

\subsection{Explicit-INCREMENT Pilot (Exploratory)}
A separate evaluation used 13 independent seeds for one configuration: 1,000 rows/operations and 5\% conflict density. Each trial started from equivalent fresh base databases, applied sequential local writes, then performed one batch synchronization. The explicit engine \texttt{\detokenize{INCREMENT}} and narrow direct-SQL additive path each matched the conflict-row additive oracle in all 13 trials; engine LWW scored 0\% under that same oracle. RVAR was 100\% for each system's observed \texttt{\detokenize{bench_rows.value}} vectors. This demonstrates selected operation-model behavior for this workload, not full database convergence or a general ranking of policies.

The paired engine-INCREMENT minus direct-SQL-SUM batch-sync delta had median 409.585 ms, inclusive IQR 406.599–424.612 ms, and range 389.840–450.654 ms (\texttt{\detokenize{n=13}}). The direct SQL implementation lacks SemanticSync's durable operation identities, replay receipts, conflict persistence, quarantine, and checkpoint behavior; therefore these paired differences are not a fair end-to-end overhead comparison. No significance test or general performance claim is made. Raw trials and the validator-derived summary are retained under \texttt{\detokenize{research/experiments/runs/increment-pilot-1000-5pct-20261020-final/}}, \texttt{\detokenize{research/experiments/runs/increment-pilot-1000-5pct-20261020-10-seeds/}}, and \texttt{\detokenize{research/experiments/derived/increment-pilot-1000-5pct-13-independent-seeds-20261020/}}.

The 13-seed pilot covers one configuration only; it does not validate the full workload matrix or establish arbitrary-order convergence.

\subsection{Key Findings}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Historical conflict-row semantic accuracy:} For this generated additive SUM oracle, LWW scores 0\% because the selected single-replica value omits one of the two deltas. This workload-specific result does not establish general SemanticSync superiority. The older single-trial results remain historical; the corrected repeated SET-based matrix remains subject to the equal-assignment intent mismatch. RVAR compares benchmark-table values only.
\item \textbf{Timing:} These sequential runs do not establish contention behavior, statistical significance, or general performance scaling. The two-trial ranges, especially at 5,000 operations/20\% density and 10,000 operations/20\% density, show that timing is variable on this host.
\item \textbf{Stress Timing Scope:} The matrix reports tracked-write measurements and batch-sync deltas; batch synchronization is not isolated policy-execution cost. The former domain loop timing is unvalidated and excluded from empirical results.
\item \textbf{Explicit operation intent:} In one 1k/5\% configuration, 13 explicit-INCREMENT trials matched the additive oracle. The paired direct-SQL timing is not an end-to-end baseline, and no result establishes a general performance advantage or arbitrary-schedule convergence.
\end{enumerate}
\section{Related Work and Scope of Comparison}
CRDT theory defines convergence conditions for specified replicated types; the framework and machine-checked examples in Shapiro et al. \cite{shapiro2011} and Gomes et al. \cite{gomes2017} are formal results for their models, not evidence about SemanticSync. Kleppmann and Beresford \cite{kleppmann2017json} give a formal JSON CRDT, and Kleppmann et al. \cite{localfirst2019} describe local-first design principles. SemanticSync is an implementation artifact with selected pairwise policies and schedules; it is not established as a CRDT by this work.

Work on invariants under weak consistency provides important prior approaches. Balegas et al. \cite{balegas2015} develop an escrow-inspired bounded counter; Balegas et al. \cite{balegas2018} present static analysis and operation changes for invariant preservation; Lewchenko et al. \cite{lewchenko2018} introduce consistency guards for conflict-aware replicated data types. SemanticSync's \texttt{\detokenize{BOUNDED_SUM}} is a bounded tracked signed-delta path with pending rejection, not an escrow allocation protocol or a formal invariant-preserving analysis. Similarity of goals does not establish novelty or equivalence.

Relational replication research includes Synql \cite{synql2024}, which composes CRDT primitives and compensates user modifications in its model, and CRDV \cite{crdv2025}, which defines replicated relation types and merge semantics through SQL views. Work on eventually consistent replicated relations and updatable views provides another CRDT-based relational model \cite{thomassen2023}. Antidote SQL \cite{antidotesql2018} studies SQL-style access to weakly consistent data. Riffle \cite{riffle2023} presents a local-first application-state architecture based on client-side relational data and reactive relational queries; it is adjacent relational work, but its focus is not SemanticSync's schema-assigned conflict resolution. These systems have distinct models and guarantees; this manuscript does not claim a feature-complete comparison. Their existence means SemanticSync cannot claim SQL-expressed merge logic or integrity-aware relational replication as a unique idea based on this selective review. The \texttt{\detokenize{cr-sqlite}} project and ElectricSQL are software artifacts describing other replication architectures \cite{crsqlite,electricsql}.

Work on relational integrity and safe local-first behavior includes history forking for data-integrity conflicts \cite{schiefer2022}, ConLoc's invariant enforcement \cite{conloc2025}, and the verified programming model in LoRe \cite{lore2024}. Borrego et al. formalize a coordination-free approach that addresses convergence and invariants in its own model \cite{borrego2025}. These works address broader formal or system-level guarantees than SemanticSync's tested, enumerated mutation paths. Edwards, Petricek, and van der Storm \cite{edwards2023} present schema-change challenge problems; that work is not evidence for foreign-key orphan handling. Spanner and Dynamo \cite{spanner2013,dynamo2007} illustrate other consistency and availability trade-offs, not systems that can be dismissed as incompatible with local-first applications on the basis of this prototype.

This related-work discussion is selective, not a systematic literature review. The repository does not establish a novelty claim for schema-level policies, quarantine, or checkpointing. A submission would need a broader, current comparison against relational sync systems, invariant-preserving replication, conflict-resolution frameworks, and application-specific merge systems.

\section{Discussion and Known Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Policy algebra:} Resolver tests cover finite generated inputs and concrete examples. \texttt{\detokenize{SUM}}'s arithmetic laws apply to fixed-base deltas under exact arithmetic, not arbitrary repeated merge histories. \texttt{\detokenize{AVG}} is pairwise and non-associative. \texttt{\detokenize{APPEND}} retains no contribution identity. LWW's tie rule is deterministic for supplied metadata, not causal. No policy set has a general arbitrary-schedule convergence proof.
\item \textbf{Operation propagation:} Synchronization is explicit and pairwise. It preserves or rejects selected operations based on expected row state and stable identity; local divergence is surfaced for review. No network transport or general offline-history merge is implemented.
\item \textbf{Relational constraints:} Quarantine applies only to enumerated tracked delete paths and supported key/reference shapes. Composite parent keys and \texttt{\detokenize{WITHOUT ROWID}} dependent rows are rejected. Parent-key updates follow declared SQLite actions, not quarantine. SQL-console and external-process writes are not tracked or quarantined; external connections may disable FK enforcement. Multi-process coordination is unsupported.
\item \textbf{Checkpoint recovery:} Production pruning requires exact ACK evidence and payload hashes, complete current membership, committed validated manifest/archive/snapshots, validated replica histories and tails, durable ACK reconciliation, dependency closure, strict HLC cutoff, and per-replica transactions. The low-level compaction helper trusts its caller-supplied eligibility set and cutoff. RESOLVE tails require manual review. Coordination uses a single-process lock; power-loss durability is not certified.
\item \textbf{Benchmark scope:} The repeated stress matrix has two paired trials per configuration, seed 42, and alternated execution order. It uses sequential tracked writes followed by batch synchronization. SCR is conflict-only against an additive oracle. Every saved SemanticSync mismatch is associated with equal competing assignments, which the current state-diff detector does not treat as a conflict even though the workload oracle counts both contributions; the reported SCR range is therefore specific to this operation model. The 0\% LWW result follows from the additive oracle and does not establish general SemanticSync superiority. RVAR compares only \texttt{\detokenize{bench_rows.value}}; VRA is unmeasured. Timing ranges are wide in some configurations, system load is uncontrolled, and results do not establish statistical significance or general performance advantage. The current domain API returns \texttt{\detokenize{illustrative_unvalidated}} with comparative metrics null. The former resolver-only proxy and hard-coded convergence outputs are excluded from empirical results.
\item \textbf{Phase evidence:} Phase 6 remains approved for its documented tested scope. Its original user-reported Windows/Python 3.14 evidence was 41 focused checkpoint tests and 309 backend tests; later local research runs completed \texttt{\detokenize{test_log_compaction.py}} with 42 passed in 4.41 s and full backend suites with 332 passed (77.43 s and 78.23 s), 334 passed (81.22 s), 335 passed (83.94 s), and most recently 336 passed with one warning (85.15 s). These commands ran on a dirty worktree and do not broaden the Phase 6 approval. They do not certify power-loss durability, multi-process coordination, every crash boundary, or arbitrary-schedule convergence.
\end{enumerate}
\section{Conclusion}
This artifact implements selected schema-level merge policies, tracked operation handling, relational quarantine paths, and checkpoint-backed log pruning for SQLite replicas. The tests and benchmark artifacts provide evidence only for their enumerated policies, schemas, inputs, and schedules. The stress matrix reports conflict-only correctness and benchmark-row agreement for a small, sequential workload; it does not demonstrate full-database convergence, concurrent-writer performance, statistical significance, or a general performance advantage. Broader correctness and comparative claims require formal specifications, wider schedule testing, reproducible domain runs, and a systematic literature comparison.
